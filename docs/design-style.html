Section 02 / Design compass

Design Style

A compact, retro strategy-management game where the player rules a vulnerable society through menus, maps, reports, and difficult decisions.

The short version

The presentation should evoke an unusually polished late-era Commodore 64 game: low-resolution pixel art, a limited palette, strong iconography, short text, simple animation, and atmospheric sound. The simulation may be deeper than the graphics suggest, but the player interacts through clear, finite choices rather than spreadsheet-heavy administration.

A small world with readable rules, scarce resources, human consequences, and enough systemic variation for every reign or expedition to tell a different story.

Shared design DNA

1. The player is a ruler, not a unit

The player makes policy, allocation, diplomatic, logistical, and moral decisions. Individuals and units matter, but the player leads the society as a whole.

2. Strategy comes from interlocking pressures

Food, population, morale, wealth, security, loyalty, territory, and time push against one another. Improvement in one area often creates cost, risk, or delayed consequences elsewhere.

3. Decisions are few, legible, and consequential

The interface can be simple enough for a joystick or a handful of keys. Depth emerges from consequences and combinations, not many nearly identical commands.

4. The world answers back

Weather, rivals, terrain, scarcity, migration, advisors, factions, and events prevent a perfect static build order and produce stories through systemic reactions.

5. People are more than a resource counter

Population loss, hunger, displacement, loyalty, and survival are tangible through reports, names, portraits, petitions, and memorial summaries.

6. Modest presentation, strong atmosphere

Use a limited palette, chunky pixels, icon-driven panels, compact maps, bitmap typography, sparse animation, and memorable sound. Retro is a discipline, not an excuse for old frustrations.

7. Sessions produce a history

Every campaign creates a concise chronicle of settlements founded, winters survived, factions betrayed, battles lost, reforms made, and causes of collapse.

The intended blend

  1. Read a compact state report.
  2. Inspect the map, settlement, people, and threats.
  3. Allocate scarce resources or issue a few orders.
  4. Resolve a dilemma, negotiation, or tactical encounter.
  5. Advance time.
  6. See consequences reshape the next turn.

The game sits between a survival settlement manager, a faction simulator, a light map-based tactical game, and a procedural narrative generator.

Style guardrails

  • Simple graphics, not shallow systems.
  • Deep consequences, not constant micromanagement.
  • Readable numbers, not opaque calculation.
  • Short text with character, not walls of exposition.
  • Focused scope, not feature sprawl.
  • Tactical conflict as one tool, not the whole game.
  • Retro immediacy with modern clarity.
  • Variation that does not erase planning.
  • Grounded stakes with dry humor and wonder.

Working design statement

A low-resolution strategy chronicle about leading a fragile people through scarcity and conflict. The player governs through compact menus, maps, petitions, and turn-based encounters. Every choice alters resources, relationships, and the society itself, producing a short but memorable history of survival, growth, compromise, or collapse.
